## Why people look beyond Notion

Notion is good at what it does, and some old complaints no longer hold. It has worked offline in its desktop and mobile apps since August 2025.

Three reasons still push people away. **Your data lives in Notion's cloud**, in a format only Notion opens directly. **You can't self-host it.** And **the Free plan gets tight for teams**: once two or more people share a Free workspace, it's limited to 1,000 blocks in total, and deleting blocks doesn't lower the count.

## AppFlowy is the closest thing to Notion

<ToolFacts tool="AppFlowy" price="Free; Pro $10/user/mo, yearly" runsOn="Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, web" license="AGPL-3.0 (app)" />

If you want Notion without Notion, start here. AppFlowy uses the same ideas: block-based pages, slash commands, and databases you can view as a grid, board or calendar. The desktop app works fully offline in local mode, with no account. You need an account to sync between devices.

The cloud Free plan includes one shared workspace with up to 2 members and 5 GB of storage. Pro costs $10 per user per month yearly, or $12.50 monthly, and allows up to 50 members.

Self-hosting has changed. The self-hosted server now comes from AppFlowy's commercial self-host repository. Its free tier covers one user seat. Team self-hosting costs $10 per seat per month, billed yearly. The app itself stays AGPL-3.0.

## AFFiNE has the most generous free team plan

<ToolFacts tool="AFFiNE" price="Free; Pro $6.75/mo; Team $10/seat/mo" runsOn="Windows, macOS, Linux, web" license="MIT editor, source-available server" />

AFFiNE mixes documents and a whiteboard in one workspace, so it also covers part of what Miro does. The Free cloud plan includes 10 GB of storage, real-time collaboration with up to 3 members per workspace and 7 days of history. Local workspaces are unlimited. Pro is $6.75 a month for 100 GB and up to 10 members.

Read the license before self-hosting. The editor and most of the code are MIT. The self-hosted backend uses AFFiNE's own source-available license: you can read it, but modifying or redistributing it needs permission. Self-hosting is free for up to 10 seats per workspace.

## Obsidian if the notes are only yours

<ToolFacts tool="Obsidian" price="Free; Sync from $4/mo, yearly" runsOn="Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android" license="Proprietary, free for any use" />

Obsidian isn't a team wiki, and that's fine if your notes are personal. It stores everything as Markdown files in a folder on your disk, it's free for any use including work, and since version 1.9 its Bases plugin gives you table, card and Kanban views over your notes. The official Importer plugin converts a Notion export into Markdown.

The catch for a list titled "open source": Obsidian is closed source. Your files are fully open, but the app isn't. For open-source options in the same style, read our [Obsidian alternatives](/obsidian-alternatives/).

## Anytype is built around privacy

<ToolFacts tool="Anytype" price="Free; paid memberships add storage" runsOn="Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android" license="Source-available (Any Source Available License)" />

Anytype keeps your data on your devices, encrypts it end to end and syncs it peer to peer. Every user gets a free remote backup node with limited storage. Only files like images and videos count toward that limit. Text and objects don't. Paid memberships add storage and more shared spaces. It's free with no limits if you run your own sync network.

Anytype's data model is different from Notion's: everything is an object with a type and relations. It's flexible, but it takes a while to learn. Its clients are source-available, not open source. You can read the code, but the license limits commercial use outside networks Anytype authorizes.

## How I'd switch without losing a single doc

I use Notion for client-facing project docs, and I've helped teams look at alternatives. The mistake is moving everything at once. Run a two-week pilot instead: pick one team, export their part of the workspace, import it into the new tool and let them do their real work there.

During the pilot, check three things. Did databases and their relations survive the import, or did they turn into flat tables? Do the integrations you rely on, like calendar, Slack or GitHub, have an equivalent? And can you export everything back out as Markdown and CSV? If a tool fails the last check, it's just a different lock-in.

## FAQ

### Is there a free open-source Notion alternative?

Yes. AppFlowy's app is open source under AGPL-3.0 and has a free cloud plan for two members. AFFiNE's editor is MIT, though its self-hosted server uses a source-available license.

### Can I self-host a Notion alternative for my team?

AFFiNE is free to self-host for up to 10 seats per workspace. AppFlowy's self-hosted server is free for one seat and then costs $10 per seat per month, billed yearly.

### Does Notion work offline now?

Yes. Since August 2025 you can mark pages as available offline in the desktop and mobile apps. It doesn't work in the browser.
