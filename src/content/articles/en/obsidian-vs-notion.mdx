## Where your notes live

This is the decision that matters. **Obsidian** stores every note as a Markdown file in a folder on your computer, called a vault. Any text editor can open those files, and they stay usable if Obsidian disappears. **Notion** stores pages in its cloud. That's what makes real-time collaboration and shared permissions easy.

Two things changed recently, so older comparisons get them wrong. Since February 2025, Obsidian is free for work, with no license required. And since August 2025, Notion works offline in its desktop and mobile apps.

## Obsidian keeps your notes as files you own

<ToolFacts tool="Obsidian" price="Free; Sync from $4/mo, yearly" runsOn="Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android" license="Proprietary, free for any use" />

The app is free for personal, commercial and non-profit use. You pay only for optional services. Sync starts at $4 a month on a yearly plan, or $5 monthly. Sync Plus is $8 a month yearly. Publish costs $8 per site per month yearly. You can also sync your vault for free with iCloud, Syncthing or Git, because it's just a folder.

Since version 1.9, the Bases core plugin turns a set of notes into a database view: table, cards, list or Kanban. The data is still in your files, as properties at the top of each note:

```markdown
---
type: book
status: reading
author: Cal Newport
rating: 4
---

Notes on chapter 3...
```

A Base can then show every note where `type` is `book` as a table, sorted by `rating`. Moving from Notion? The official Importer plugin converts a Notion export to Markdown files.

## Notion is built for shared work

<ToolFacts tool="Notion" price="Free; Plus $10/member/mo, yearly" runsOn="Web, Windows, macOS, iOS, Android" license="Proprietary" />

Notion combines pages, databases and comments in one shared workspace. Several people can edit a page at the same time, and you control who sees what. Plus costs $10 per member per month on a yearly plan, or $12 monthly. Business is $20 per member per month, yearly.

The Free plan works well alone. When two or more people share a Free workspace, it has a limit of 1,000 blocks in total, and deleting blocks doesn't reduce the count. Free also limits uploads to 5 MB per file and keeps 7 days of page history.

Offline works in the desktop and mobile apps, not in the browser. You mark pages as available offline, one device at a time. Databases download their first 50 rows. On paid plans, recent and favorite pages download automatically.

## Why I use both

I use Obsidian for my own notes: technical snippets, project logs, things I'm learning. I use Notion for anything a client or a team needs to see. Trying to make one tool do both jobs is where most people get frustrated.

If you go with Obsidian and want free sync, a Git repo works well for text-heavy vaults. Keep the per-device files out of it, or you'll get conflicts every time you switch machines:

```text
.obsidian/workspace.json
.obsidian/workspace-mobile.json
.trash/
```

On Notion, the habit that pays off is structure from day one: one database for docs with an owner and a "last reviewed" date. A wiki without owners turns stale in months, whatever tool it lives in.

## How to choose in 30 seconds

- **Personal notes, research, journaling:** Obsidian.
- **No subscription, ever:** Obsidian, with your own sync.
- **A team wiki or shared project docs:** Notion.
- **You want both:** Obsidian for your own thinking, Notion for team docs.

## FAQ

### Is Obsidian free for commercial use?

Yes. Since February 2025, Obsidian is free for work. The commercial license, $50 per user per year, is optional for companies that want to support the project.

### Can I use Notion offline?

Yes, since August 2025, in the desktop and mobile apps. You turn on "Available offline" for the pages you need. Paid plans also download recent and favorite pages automatically.

### Can I move from Notion to Obsidian?

Yes. Export your Notion workspace and use Obsidian's official Importer plugin. It converts pages to Markdown files in your vault.
