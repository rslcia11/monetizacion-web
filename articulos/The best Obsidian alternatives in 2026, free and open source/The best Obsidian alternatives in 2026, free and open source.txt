Our picks
Why teams look for alternatives
Obsidian is an excellent local-first application. On February 20, 2025, the company updated its license to make the app completely free for commercial use. You no longer need to pay to use it at your job.   

However, the core application remains proprietary and closed-source. You cannot inspect the code. Many users want a guarantee that their primary knowledge base will never be locked behind a paywall or sold to another company. This pushes them toward fully open-source alternatives. If you do not care about open-source code and just want a cloud-based wiki, you can also check out Obsidian vs Notion.

Logseq: the open-source outliner
Logseq is an open-source knowledge management tool. It uses a block-based structure where every paragraph is a separate node. You can link these blocks together to create a web of connected ideas, making it function as an outliner.   

Like Obsidian, your data stays on your local device as plain text Markdown files. You can read and edit these files with any basic text editor. The source code is publicly available under the AGPL-3.0 license, ensuring anyone can audit it.

Joplin: folders and free sync
Joplin organizes information using traditional notebooks and tags. It provides an interface that feels very familiar if you are moving away from older tools like Evernote. It also includes end-to-end encryption to secure your data during transfers.   

The desktop and mobile apps are free and open-source. You can sync your notes across devices without paying by connecting the app to your existing Dropbox, Nextcloud, or WebDAV accounts. If you want a managed service instead, they sell a Joplin Cloud subscription.

How to choose in 30 seconds
You want a block-based outliner: Logseq.

You prefer standard folders and tags: Joplin.

You want built-in sync via Dropbox: Joplin.

You just want to use Obsidian for work: Stay with Obsidian, it is now free for commercial use.

FAQ
Is Obsidian free for commercial use?
Yes. In February 2025, Obsidian updated its terms to allow free commercial use. You can use the application at work without buying a license.   

Does Logseq store data locally?
Yes. Logseq is a local-first application. It saves your notes as plain Markdown files directly on your hard drive, not on a remote server.   

Can I sync Joplin for free?
Yes. Joplin allows you to sync your notes across devices for free using third-party services like Dropbox, OneDrive, Nextcloud, or WebDAV.
