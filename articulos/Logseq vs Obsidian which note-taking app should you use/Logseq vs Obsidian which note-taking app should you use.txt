The verdict
The outliner vs the folder
Both Logseq and Obsidian are local-first applications that store your data as plain Markdown files on your hard drive. You never have to worry about a cloud server going down or a vendor locking you out of your account.   

The main difference is how they structure information. Obsidian acts like a traditional file manager. You create a note, put it in a folder, and write in it. Logseq is an outliner. Instead of writing long standard documents, you write in bullet points. Every bullet point (or block) becomes a node that you can link to other notes across your graph.

Logseq: the open-source outliner
Logseq is an open-source knowledge management tool. It uses an outliner format where every paragraph or bullet point is an individual block. This allows for precise bidirectional linking between specific thoughts rather than just linking whole pages.   

The application saves all your data locally as plain text files. It is completely free for personal and commercial use, and its core code is available under the AGPL-3.0 license. This ensures that your knowledge base is not tied to a proprietary, closed system.

Obsidian: folders and flexibility
Obsidian is also a local-first application that saves your notes as Markdown files. However, it takes a more traditional approach. You create files, place them in folders, and write standard long-form text. You can still use bidirectional links to connect pages, but the foundation feels more like a standard text editor.

The application is closed-source, meaning you cannot audit its code. However, as of February 2025, Obsidian is completely free for both personal and commercial use. You no longer need to buy a license to use it at your job.

How to choose in 30 seconds
You want to outline your thoughts in bullet points: Logseq.

You want open-source software to avoid vendor lock-in: Logseq.

You want to write long-form articles or journal entries: Obsidian.

You want to organize notes using standard folders: Obsidian.

FAQ
Is Obsidian free for work?
Yes. In February 2025, Obsidian changed its license rules. The application is now free for all commercial use, meaning you can use it at your company without purchasing a commercial license.   

Does Logseq store my notes in the cloud?
No. By default, Logseq is a local-first application. It saves your data as plain text files on your local hard drive. It does not force you to upload your notes to a cloud server.   

Is Logseq open source?
Yes. Logseq is an open-source project published under the AGPL-3.0 license. Anyone can inspect the code, modify it, and run it independently.
