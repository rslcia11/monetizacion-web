Our picks
Why teams are leaving Firebase
Firebase offers a generous free tier, but the transition to its paid tier introduces risk. The Blaze plan operates strictly on a pay-as-you-go model. You pay for every database read, write, and cloud function execution above the free limits. There is no way to set a hard spending cap. If your app has an inefficient query loop, your monthly bill can jump from zero to hundreds of dollars overnight.   

Many teams look for alternatives to get predictable flat-rate billing. They also want open-source solutions that prevent vendor lock-in, since Firebase is entirely proprietary and managed by Google.

Supabase: PostgreSQL with compute pricing
Supabase is an open-source alternative that replaces Firebase's NoSQL document store with a PostgreSQL database. This allows you to write standard SQL queries and use relational data models.   

The Pro plan costs $25 per month per organization, but that is only the base fee. Every project runs on its own compute instance, which is billed separately. The base plan includes a $10 credit covering one small compute instance, but scaling up to handle more traffic will increase your monthly bill. The Pro plan includes a spend cap by default to prevent unexpected overages.

Appwrite: flat fees and high bandwidth
Appwrite is an open-source backend that gives you options. As of the 2.0 release in 2026, it supports native PostgreSQL and MySQL alongside its default database.   

Appwrite stands out for its predictable pricing. It charges a flat $25 per month per project. This base price includes a massive 2TB of API bandwidth. Unlike Firebase, you do not pay per database read or write within the tier limits, making it much easier to budget for high-traffic applications.

How to choose in 30 seconds
You want relational data and a huge ecosystem: Supabase.

You want a flat monthly fee with massive bandwidth: Appwrite.

You need to self-host to avoid Google lock-in: Supabase or Appwrite.

You want to stay on a pay-as-you-go model: Stay with Firebase.

FAQ
Can I self-host Firebase?
No. Firebase is a proprietary, closed-source service managed entirely by Google. If you want to self-host your backend, you must switch to an open-source alternative like Supabase or Appwrite.   

Is Supabase cheaper than Firebase?
It depends on your scale. Firebase has a larger free tier for NoSQL document reads, but its Blaze plan charges for every operation. Supabase charges a flat $25 organization fee plus compute costs, which is more expensive for a small prototype but more predictable for a scaling application.   

Does Appwrite support SQL?
Yes. As of the Appwrite 2.0 update in 2026, the platform natively supports PostgreSQL and MySQL databases. You can manage these relational databases directly inside your Appwrite project.
